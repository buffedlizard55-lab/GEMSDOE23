The question the brief asked

What do local H19-like artifacts suggest—and what remains unverified?

Decompose the locally audited files into the three things the metric actually rewards. This is file-level analysis only: the public score rows do not identify these TIFFs, and the correlations do not prove a causal feature gain.

It put its pixels where the hidden faults are

h19-5 achieves q = 0.0475 kernel-weighted true positives per emitted pixel — the second highest of 24 artefacts, behind only h28-dotted-ridge at 0.0518. Since DTI correlates with q at ρ = +0.987, that single number is the score. Inverted exactly, h19-5 recovered TPw ≈ 5,749 of a hidden truth set of order 104 pixels from 121,131 emitted pixels: roughly 21 hits per 1,000 emitted pixels, against 1.8 per 1,000 for a uniform-random placement.

It stayed off the masked catalogue

h19-5, h19-4, h16-1 and lidarscarp-top2pct emit zero pixels on known-fault pixels, which staff mask out of evaluation. The artefacts that painted the catalogue (h19-c, structural-area06-v1, hedge-v2: 60,988 on-catalogue pixels each) score 0.156 or less, and r5-geom-horse-ensemble, whose every pixel lies within 1,500 m of a mapped fault, scores 0.0020 — 96× worse than h19-5 at a similar budget.

It kept a moderate budget

At |G| ≈ 104 each emitted pixel costs ≈ 0.197 of denominator, so the false-negative floor 0.8·|G| ≈ 8,000 is comparable to the FP term only up to A ≈ 105. h19-5 sat at 121,131 px; every artefact above 250,000 px scores ≤ 0.046. The score is not a coverage contest.

What the arrangement audit adds to the H19 answer. h19-5 sits at signed divergence -0.29 from the catalogue's pair statistics (h19-4 −0.30, h16-1 −0.29), the centre of the bin that holds the best live scores (n = 7, mean live DTI 0.170); it has no 400 m survey-line comb (strength 2), a line-type arrangement (10 % isolated dots) and only mild concentration near long known faults (×1.37 within 0.2–1 km, against ×2.8–4.3 for the catalogue-hugging artefacts that scored ≤ 0.092). The lattices (−0.62) and the catalogue-huggers (+0.2 … +1.25) match the population statistics of faults worse. That is consistent with the arithmetic above; it does not prove it (correlational, post-hoc bins, n = 23).
Where H19 might have left value on the table — and why that is no longer a claim. Its dispersion efficiency is η = 0.40: 60 % of the cone weight its pixels could have spread over the domain is spent on pixels stacked inside one another's 300 m support. H24 re-emitted the same ranking at η ≈ 0.95 on the assumption that q scales with η at fixed alignment. The one controlled pair in the group's record contradicts that: pindrop-v4-ridge → pindrop-v4-nodes (same score, same 155,021 pixels) raised η 2.7× and left the implied true-positive mass unchanged (TP/|G| 0.70 → 0.72 at |G| = 6,000), live DTI 0.1152 → 0.1193. H30 has no observed score. The scenario table is conditional on unverified transfer of placement skill; it is not an expected DTI or evidence for release (flag I-17).

Why h19-5 beat h19-4 — and what beating the leaders takes

The two H19 emissions differ only in their four evidence-line weights and budget (2.45 % vs 2.50 %). Their rasters overlap at Jaccard 0.777: 107,106 shared pixels, 14,025 only in h19-5, 16,673 only in h19-4. The +0.0028 live delta therefore measures which ground the re-weighting moved mass onto. Full record: h19-placement-analysis.json.

The delta rewards specific scarp morphology

h19-5-only pixels sit on stronger lid_downface_max (+0.027), lid_step_max (+0.025), of_det_elev_slope (+0.025), lid_relief (+0.023) and gravity gradients (of_iso_grav_anom_std7 +0.022) than h19-4-only pixels, and on weaker generic local roughness (of_det_elev_slope_std7 −0.037) and weaker sgmc_gap_density9 (−0.017). Read: the hidden truth prefers oriented, specific scarp steps and relief over diffuse roughness, and does not prefer ground dense in SGMC-only faults.

All four top artefacts share one signature

Enrichment of every top-4 emission vs the domain: proximity to catalogue faults (geom_inv_d_catalogue +0.52…+0.70) and to SGMC structure (+0.22…+0.55), on steep rough ground (of_det_elev_slope_std7 +0.17…+0.50) with strong scarps (lid_upface_max +0.22…+0.40) — while avoiding the catalogue itself (all four emit exactly zero pixels on the masked catalogue; median distance to the nearest catalogue pixel 0.86–1.49 km). The hidden faults behave exactly as the staff ruling describes: newly mapped geometry near existing systems.

The leaders' scores are only feasible at large |G|

At perfect recall an emission of area A caps at DTI = |G|/(|G| + 0.197·A). 0.3049–0.3195 is unreachable for h19-5's budget (121k px) unless |G| ≥ ~12,000, and even at H30's smaller, more efficient budget (91.5k px) it needs |G| ≥ ~8,000; at H30's geometry (K̄ = 0.157, the group's most TP-efficient arrangement) the leaders require placement skill 4.3–5.7 at |G| = 10–15k — i.e. |G| near the published upper bound with recall ≳ 0.5. The leaders are finding a large fraction of a large truth set, not a small clever subset.

Can we beat 0.1922? The honest algebra. Three measured facts bound the answer: (1) h19-5's mean TP per emitted pixel (0.052 at |G| = 10k) still exceeds the marginal-payoff threshold (0.039), so it was slightly under-budgeted, not over; (2) H30's geometry is ~1.8× more TP-efficient per emitted pixel than h19-5's (K̄ 0.157 vs 0.088); (3) the placement-quality gap between h19-5 (skill 7.2 at |G| = 10k) and the group's mid-tier artefacts (ens12 5.9) is small. A submission that carries h19-5-class placement onto H30-class dispersion would clear 0.1922 at |G| ≥ 10k under the (unverified) skill-transfer assumption — but that assumption is exactly what flag I-17 warns about, no offline proxy validates it, and no slot may be spent on it. The path to beating the leaders additionally requires recall of half of a ≥10k-pixel truth set, which none of our 24 artefacts has demonstrated.
