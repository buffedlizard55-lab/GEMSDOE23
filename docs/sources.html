Claim-by-claim source register

Official pages establish the competition contract and provenance of public geospatial sources. They do not establish that a proposed model works. Links are provided for direct review; scope and evidence gaps are made explicit.

Source hierarchy: DOE/NLR rules and DrivenData pages for contest claims; USGS/ScienceBase and official government catalogues for geospatial data; NeurIPS proceedings for the ensemble paper. Team-maintained project pages are used only to preserve reported prior experiments, not as official scientific or competition evidence.

External reading for new hypotheses

  • DrivenData About page defines geological fault traces and discusses field, geophysical, and topographic detection approaches; it cites Mattéo et al. (2021) and Hermant et al. (2025).
  • Mattéo et al. (2021), automatic fault mapping in remote optical/topographic data (DOI resolves to the journal article).
  • Hermant, Kiersnowski & Bellanger (2025), deep learning to map Quaternary faults in the western U.S. (Stanford Geothermal Workshop paper).
  • NGMDB overview describes MapView and its catalog relationship; maps are compiled from multiple publication sources and scales.

Citations are starting points. Before an actual geological claim is made, retrieve the full text and record the specific finding and study-area transfer limitations in the research ledger.

Verification ledger rule

For every numeric claim in a candidate report, retain the input file SHA-256, code commit, configuration, fold definition, candidate score, comparison baseline, and source URL. For external data, retain access date, license/terms, CRS, extent, resolution, and a file checksum. Unknown values stay blank or “not verified”—never guessed.
