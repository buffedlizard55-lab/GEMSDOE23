H1 · first to validate

Multi-sensor geophysical edge-normal consensus

Layers & physical signature

The official page lists total magnetic intensity (`TMI`), reduced-to-pole magnetics, isostatic gravity, surface conductivity, and depth to conductive base. The organizer reference notebook's printed metadata instead labels a channel `Depth to basement surface`; that is not assumed to be the same product. The resolver excludes derivative bands for raw TMI/gravity and will fail closed until the downloaded raster confirms a unique depth channel. At 100–400 m smoothing scales, compute gradient magnitude and unoriented edge-normal alignment (`|cos Δθ|`) across magnetic, gravity, and conductive families; require cross-family support rather than a single sharp boundary.

Why a catalogue gap might show here

A fault may juxtapose rocks with contrasting magnetization, density, or electrical conductivity even where the surface trace is subtle or buried. Independent aligned contrasts could locate a structure not drawn in the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue. Lithologic contacts and processing edges are important false-positive alternatives.

Difference from earlier work

The linked H19 page describes a geopotential “strike worm” component and earlier pages describe geophysics/ridge ensembles. This test is different in its preregistered cross-family gradient-normal agreement and explicit one-sensor ablation; it does not simply emit magnetic or gravity ridges. Those prior descriptions have not been independently reproduced in this checkout.

Data, cost & validation

Uses official competition-provided GeoDAWN/INGENIOUS feature bands, so it needs no new external dataset; the files themselves remain login-gated. Estimated cost: medium. Freeze band-name mapping from GeoTIFF metadata, then compare a baseline U-Net/ensemble with and without the consensus channel on identical out-of-fold quadrants and equal emission budgets. Require positive mean DTI delta and at least 3/4 fold wins under the preregistered gate. No local result exists.

H2 · intended uncertainty innovation

Survey-gap-conditioned epistemic review priority

Layers & physical signature

For each candidate pixel/trace, use independently trained ensemble probabilities on the competition feature stack, plus an independent mapped-coverage proxy derived from USGS National Geologic Map Database (NGMDB) map footprints, scale, and publication metadata. High ensemble variance in sparse/low-detail mapped areas becomes a discovery cue; the same disagreement under dense, detailed coverage becomes a caution.

Why the catalogue gap might be spatially structured

The official competition states that the public fault catalogue is incomplete and may be inaccurate. It does not establish that historical fieldwork scarcity caused the omissions or identify which terrain was undersurveyed. This experiment directly tests that additional premise rather than assuming it.

Difference from earlier work

The user-supplied history describes feature fusion, model ensembles, thin-ridge emissions, and geological priors, but not an independent survey-coverage observation model interacting with epistemic uncertainty. This is a review-priority mechanism, not an uncertainty-only rejection rule and not a submission probability adjustment.

External source check & cost

USGS NGMDB MapView and the NGMDB catalog are publicly browsable and provide map records/metadata. The relevant footprints and scale metadata have not been downloaded, rasterized, or tested for this study area. Map coverage is not direct field-effort evidence. Estimated cost: high. Do not call the coverage proxy viable until a spatial layer is obtained, independently checked, and compared with survey-area records.

H3 · terrain, not another scarp score

Hydrogeomorphic drainage offsets and channel knickpoints

Layer & signature

USGS 3DEP 1 m bare-earth DEM: derive drainage paths, channel curvature, and longitudinal-profile slope breaks; measure coherent lateral channel deflection or paired knickpoints along a candidate linear structure at multiple channel orders.

Why it may find unmapped traces

A subtle fault can perturb channel course or base level without producing a clean scarp at 100 m. Requiring aligned effects on independent channels may reject isolated topographic artifacts. Lithology, drainage engineering, landslides, and DEM artifacts can imitate the signal.

Difference from earlier work

H19 pages describe 1 m/10 m topographic openness, local-relief/scarp detection, and ridge thinning. The proposed signature is network topology and along-channel profile discontinuity, not scarp polarity, openness, or ridge emission.

External source check & cost

The USGS 3DEP program states elevation products are free and links a 1 m DEM availability viewer and download tools. The exact GeoDAWN tile set is not verified here because `1m_DEM_links.csv` is absent. Cost: high (many high-resolution tiles and hydrology processing). Viable only after overlap/quality audit.

H4 · subsurface geometry

Conductive-base and magnetic-source-depth breaks

Layers & physical signature

Depth to conductive base surface, surface conductivity, and top-of-crustal magnetic source-depth estimate. Compute multiscale curvature / zero-crossings in the depth surfaces and test whether aligned discontinuities persist between electrical and magnetic estimates.

Why it may find unmapped traces

A basement fault or step may control conductor geometry and magnetic source depth even where surficial mapping has not resolved a trace. The mechanism is independent of spring/well heat anomalies. Spatial-resolution and inversion-smoothing artifacts may dominate.

Difference from earlier work

The linked H19 write-up names thermal/geochemical conduit inversion and geopotential lineaments; this candidate targets the geometry of electrical/magnetic source surfaces, not thermal targets or gravity-strike paths. Current checkout has no existing implementation.

Data, cost & validation

These feature families are listed in the official competition description; data access still requires the official data tab. No additional external dataset. Cost: medium. Validate held-out known-fault recovery at equal candidate density and run single-channel ablations to exclude a one-layer pattern match.

H5 · exploratory / lower prior

Strain-accommodation boundaries and seismicity contrast

Layers & physical signature

Dilatation rate, shear strain rate, second invariant of the strain-rate tensor, and earthquake density. Test multiscale tensor gradients and linearly coherent strain boundaries that are adjacent to—not simply colocated with—seismicity-density transitions.

Why it may find unmapped traces

A fault can accommodate distributed deformation and create a sharp strain boundary; seismicity can vary along the same structure. This may reveal a structural corridor not represented by a surface trace, but the relationship is non-unique and the provided earthquake-density window/time support must be audited.

Difference from earlier work

The proposed feature is an explicit spatial derivative/contrast between strain tensors and seismicity, not a generic classifier trained on the same channels. It does not use the H19 population-length budget or DEM scarps.

Data, cost & validation

Uses named feature families listed on the official competition page; no new external data. Estimated cost: low–medium. Require data-window provenance, directional controls, permutation/rotation nulls, and a spatially blocked comparison. Because earthquake density can proxy mapped/active systems, score only as exploratory until label leakage and survey bias are ruled out.

Decision rule

  1. Inventory the official feature-band descriptions; if the exact channel mapping is absent, stop rather than guess band positions.
  2. Freeze spatial folds, the 300 m metric support, a buffer sensitivity plan, and equal pixel budgets before looking at candidate scores.
  3. Compare each candidate against the current reproducible holdout incumbent on the same folds; include foldwise deltas and uncertainty intervals across spatial subblocks.
  4. Only candidate 1 is currently implemented as a generic edge-consensus feature function. None is validated or submission-eligible.
  5. When a survey-coverage source is missing or not spatially testable, omit H2's adjustment and report coverage as unknown.

The local code implementing H1 is src/gems/geology.py. It refuses missing layer names and has not been run on competition rasters.
