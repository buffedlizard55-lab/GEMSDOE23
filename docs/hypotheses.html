Candidate 1 · highest expected value

GeoDAWN radiometric alteration/contact edges plus independent structural support

Layers & physical transform

Use the USGS GeoDAWN Area 1/Area 2 radiometric GeoTIFF grids. Confirm exact band names, units, nodata, projection, and survey-area tags from the release metadata/ReadMe before coding. The USGS technical paper describes potassium (%K), equivalent uranium (eU), equivalent thorium (eTh), and standard radiometric ratios/ternary representations. Test robust, per-survey normalized radioelement ratios (for example K/eTh and eU/eTh where positive and valid), their multiscale gradients, and persistent line-like boundaries. Require corroboration from independently processed competition magnetics or topography; do not use radiometric texture alone. Resample only after native-resolution, CRS, mask, and flight-line QA.

Why a fault may be absent from the catalogue

Fault-controlled fluid circulation or juxtaposition of rock units can create near-surface changes in radioelement abundance; these can complement weak magnetic contrasts in basin fill. USGS authors show GeoDAWN K concentrations tracking mapped geological boundaries and varying within units previously mapped as one unit. This is an indirect surface-geology/alteration indicator—not a direct measurement of a fault. Soil moisture, cover, lithology, weathering, flight-height variation, and survey seams are strong alternative explanations.

Difference from supplied prior attempts

The supplied H19 descriptions emphasize thermal/geochemical conduit reasoning, topographic openness/local relief, and geophysical lineaments. The organizer notebook's printed 19-band inventory names magnetic, gravity, strain, seismic, topographic, and subsurface fields but no K/eU/eTh radiometric channels. That inventory is not a substitute for inspecting the gated raster's actual tags, so radiometric novelty remains conditional on checking the real input and all prior feature manifests.

Official source, cost & falsification

USGS GeoDAWN DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ is a public USGS/DOE release explicitly marked CC0. ScienceBase lists Area 1 and Area 2 GeoTIFF archives (about 44 MB and 231 MB) plus metadata/ReadMe; the contest region is described as the GeoDAWN study area. No archive was downloaded here and exact pixel coverage/alignment is unverified. Cost: medium-high. After the competition files are authorized, freeze spatial blocks/buffers and candidate density, establish a reproducible baseline, then compare this feature on the same folds and 300 m DTI support. Require a positive mean delta and wins in at least 3/4 folds under the existing gate; inspect lithology, flight-line, and mask controls. No result exists.

Candidate 2 · direct surface expression

Cross-channel drainage deflection and profile knickpoints

Layers & physical transform

Use the competition's `1m_DEM_links.csv` (when legitimately available) and matched USGS 3DEP bare-earth DEM tiles. Extract drainage networks and longitudinal profiles; measure channel deflection, slope-area/profile breaks, and knickpoint continuity across multiple independent catchments. Aggregate sub-100 m evidence to the 100 m grid without inventing subpixel precision. Condition on mapped lithology, channel order, contributing area, and DEM quality.

Why a fault may be absent from the catalogue

Fault-related uplift, tilting, or offset can perturb channels and generate aligned profile breaks even where no continuous scarp is visible at contest resolution. Only preserve candidates with repeated, structurally consistent evidence. Lithologic contacts, climate/base-level changes, landslides, roads, and DEM artifacts can produce similar forms.

Difference from prior attempts

The supplied H19 pages describe 1 m/10 m openness, local relief, scarp/ridge extraction, and thin ridge emission. This test is drainage-network topology and along-channel profile behavior, not another scarp, openness, or ridge score. It has not been located in the supplied H19 descriptions; broader prior-project coverage is incomplete.

Official source, availability & cost

USGS 3DEP says elevation products are free and without use restrictions. A preliminary query to the official 1 m index's “1 meter” layer using the reference raster's broad EPSG:32611 envelope returned one intersecting `dissolve` feature. It gave no tile IDs and does not prove complete contest-footprint coverage. Exact links/tiles, quality, and overlap still need audit. Cost: high because of tile QA and hydrology processing. Do not proceed unless complete enough coverage and quality controls are established.

Candidate 3 · deep regional context

Depth-coherent upper-crustal MT conductance boundaries

Layers & physical transform

Evaluate the USGS Great Basin conductance GeoTIFFs at 2–12 km, 12–20 km, and—only as a deeper control—20–50 km (the release also lists 50–90 and 90–200 km). Work at native support first. Test broad gradient/boundary continuity and whether upper-crustal conductors or transitions persist across adjacent depth bands; do not upsample regional grids and treat them as independent 100 m detail.

Why a fault may be absent from the catalogue

Fluid-rich or altered fault corridors may contribute to conductive structures that help contextualize blind crustal breaks. USGS describes the grids as information about subsurface fluids and pathways. Conductance is non-unique: saline basin fill, graphite, clay, and other conductive phases can dominate, and a regional anomaly is not a mapped fault trace.

Difference from prior attempts

The official feature inventory includes a surface-conductivity measure and a conductive-base surface; H19 descriptions also mention geophysical lineaments. This hypothesis adds a separate, multi-depth regional magnetotelluric model, not another derivative of the same competition surface layer. Verify source independence and feature provenance before using it.

Official source, availability & cost

USGS ScienceBase DOI 10.5066/P9TWT2LU lists five public downloadable GeoTIFFs. Its geographic metadata bounding box overlaps the broad Great Basin/GeoDAWN region, but exact valid-pixel overlap, raster resolution, CRS transform, and reuse terms were not checked. The files were not downloaded. Cost: medium; low confidence because regional support may limit localization. Validate only as a broad-context ablation and require the same frozen spatial-fold gate.

Research triage · separate from the DTI ranking

Epistemic disagreement plus independent map-coverage context

For each human-review candidate, independently trained ensemble members must report the mean, epistemic variance, conditional/aleatoric variance, and total variance separately. High epistemic disagreement in independently supported low-map-coverage terrain is a reason to elevate reviewer priority; similarly high disagreement in well-mapped terrain deserves extra scrutiny. The coverage source is an NGMDB map-footprint/scale proxy, not fieldwork truth. This is a reviewer-priority hypothesis, not a direct DTI feature, not a probability correction, and not permission to modify the one-band submission raster. No coverage layer or trained ensemble exists here; see the uncertainty handoff contract.

Screened out before implementation

Promising sources that do not yet justify a new branch

  • USGS regional gravity/magnetic/depth-to-basement grids (P9Z6SA1Z): public 6.5 MB grid archive, but the main layers overlap gravity/magnetic/basement feature families already supplied for the competition. Likely adds little independent information; not ranked without a clear provenance/scale distinction.
  • USGS Quaternary-fault slip/dilation tendency (P9YL58W6): public shapefile/KMZ products calculate tendency for faults already in the public fault database. They do not directly locate unknown faults and overlap the mechanical/wing-crack direction in the supplied H20-1 history; using them as new labels would risk circularity.
  • INGENIOUS/GDR 1391: public CC BY 4.0 compilation with thermal, geodetic, fault, and well/spring data, but much of that rationale overlaps the H19 thermal/geochemical and geophysical prior. No new candidate is advanced solely because the dataset is available.

The full source register records dataset scope, availability, and caveats. Public listing is not evidence of local download, correct alignment, permitted derivative use, or predictive value.

Frozen validation and stop rule

  1. Obtain competition inputs only through authorized DrivenData access; inspect actual band descriptions, nodata, transform, masks, and label encoding. Do not guess band indexes.
  2. Before feature work, freeze spatially blocked folds and buffers, the official DTI support/implementation, candidate-emission budget, transforms, ablations, and pass threshold. Establish a reproducible baseline first; none exists yet.
  3. Test rank 1 first against that baseline on identical folds. Report every fold, mean delta, uncertainty, and lithology/flight-line controls. A public leaderboard score cannot replace this test.
  4. Only if rank 1 clears the existing spatial-validation gate may it be considered for a weekly upload. Otherwise retain the baseline and test the next hypothesis without spending a slot.
  5. Keep the previously implemented edge-consensus function (`src/gems/geology.py`) as an unscored diagnostic/ablation only; it is not the new top-ranked hypothesis or a validated incumbent.
