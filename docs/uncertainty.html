Every candidate gets an uncertainty explanation

Model disagreement can be a finding

A fault score alone cannot tell a geologist whether the network is reading a weak but repeatable signal or extrapolating because it lacks evidence. This design reports the variance split and an independently sourced survey-coverage context for every candidate.

Not yet measured on competition data. The decomposition functions and model scaffold are in this repository, but no ensemble has been trained because the official rasters are unavailable. No actual epistemic map, aleatoric map, or Phase 2 candidate report is claimed.
Deep ensemble, not MC dropout

Train independent members; infer each deterministically

Train M separate networks from independently sampled initial weights, each optimized independently using the same training set and independent data-loader/random seeds. No member reuses another member's weights or optimizer state. The shipped U-Net has no dropout layer; inference runs with evaluation mode and no gradient. The method is motivated by Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel & Blundell, Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation Using Deep Ensembles, NeurIPS 2017.

The current default config requests five members. This is a starting compute budget, not a statistically established optimum. Members should be calibrated out-of-fold before the probabilities are described as calibrated confidence.

Variance decomposition

Exact law of total variance for the ensemble mixture

For model member m, let pm(x) be that member's probability of fault presence at pixel x. View the ensemble as a uniform mixture over member identity, followed by a Bernoulli outcome.

p̄ = Em[pm]
Uepi = Varm(pm)
Ualea = Em[pm(1 − pm)]
Utotal = p̄(1 − p̄) = Uepi + Ualea
Epistemic

Between-model disagreement

High variance means independently fitted members assign different probabilities. It can signal lack of evidence, model sensitivity, distribution shift, or optimization instability. It is not proof that the terrain was under-surveyed.

Aleatoric / conditional

Within-member Bernoulli ambiguity

For members that each predict a probability near 0.5, the conditional Bernoulli variance is high even when the ensemble agrees. It may reflect irreducible ambiguity, label uncertainty, or miscalibration. Call it an aleatoric estimate—not proven geological randomness—until calibration and the observation process are evaluated.

The implementation uses population variance (`ddof=0`) because it treats the finite ensemble as a uniformly weighted predictive mixture. `src/gems/uncertainty.py` checks the identity numerically and rejects invalid probabilities.

Why survey coverage changes triage

Use disagreement differently where mapping effort differs

Let c(x) ∈ [0,1] be an independent mapped-coverage proxy, where 0 means less mapped coverage and 1 means more. Scale epistemic standard deviation to `[0,1]` by dividing by its Bernoulli maximum of 0.5 and clipping. Define a review-priority adjustment:

A(x) = λ · min(√Uepi(x) / 0.5, 1) · (1 − 2c(x))

With positive, validation-selected `λ`, high epistemic uncertainty at low mapped coverage raises priority. The same disagreement at high mapped coverage lowers it. At `c=0.5`, no signed adjustment is applied. If coverage is unknown, adjustment is exactly zero and the report says why.

Illustration only, not measured data: members `{0.1, 0.9}` give mean `0.5`, epistemic variance `0.16`, conditional variance `0.09`, total `0.25`. At an illustrative `λ=0.10`, normalized disagreement is `0.8`: coverage `c=0` shifts the review score by `+0.08`; coverage `c=1` shifts it by `−0.08`. The resulting `0.58`/`0.42` are ranking scores, not probabilities and not a proposed model output.

The adjustment must not silently alter the submission raster. Select `λ` only on spatially blocked folds and report sensitivity. An NGMDB map-footprint/scale index is at best a mapping-coverage proxy; it does not directly measure fieldwork intensity, and map absence may reflect digitization/cataloguing rather than lack of geological work.

The script scripts/export_candidate_review.py exports per-component averages from candidate-label and uncertainty maps. It records a centroid in the template CRS. The single-band competition GeoTIFF remains the mean probability or separately selected submission score; reviewers receive epistemic/aleatoric rasters and the CSV as companion materials.

Safeguards

What this does not establish

  • A high epistemic map is not, by itself, a map of sparse fieldwork.
  • A high conditional Bernoulli variance is not automatically irreducible geological ambiguity.
  • Ensemble uncertainty is not calibrated until checked with out-of-fold reliability/error analyses and spatial distribution-shift tests.
  • The official known-fault raster is an incomplete catalogue, so a background zero is not necessarily a true negative. Training code downweights unlabeled background; the effect must be tested.
  • Phase 2 candidate reporting is a scientific handoff proposal. The competition's official one-band GeoTIFF submission contract remains unchanged.
