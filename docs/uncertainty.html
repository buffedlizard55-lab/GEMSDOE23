Epistemic versus aleatoric, from a deep ensemble

The prior run used 5 independently initialised, independently trained convolutional members; no test-time dropout. The checkpoint, training rasters and external coverage layers are not present in this checkout, so these saved candidate variances are not freshly reproduced or calibrated here. For Bernoulli outputs the total predictive variance decomposes exactly as Var(Y) = Em[pm(1 − pm)] + Varm(pm) — aleatoric first, epistemic second (population variance, ddof = 0) — per Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel & Blundell, NeurIPS 2017.

The brief's rule, implemented literally

How survey coverage changes a candidate's priority

High epistemic variance in under-surveyed terrain → a finding

The members disagree because the data does not constrain the answer. Where independent survey coverage is low, that is exactly where a structure can exist and never have been recorded, so the disagreement raises review priority. 14 of the reported candidates fall in this class.

High epistemic variance in well-surveyed terrain → suspicion

If the ground has been flown, lidar-mapped and geologically mapped and the members still disagree, the disagreement is more likely a model artefact than a missing fault, so priority is damped. 13 candidates are flagged this way.

Aleatoric variance never changes priority

Em[pm(1 − pm)] is irreducible noise in the observations — 100 m pixels mixing a 3 m scarp with its footwall, 400 m flight-line spacing, misregistered source maps. Adding members cannot reduce it, so it is reported but not acted on.

Coverage is measured, not assumed

The prior run defined coverage = 0.5 · (1 m 3DEP lidar validity, band 12 of lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif, 0.754 of the footprint) + 0.5 · (rank of USGS SGMC structure-line density in a 2.1 km window). Those external rasters are absent now, so the mask and AOI validity cannot be checked from this checkout. Priority never modifies the submitted raster — it is a reviewer-ranking aid only.
