Deep Ensemble epistemic vs. aleatoric decomposition

Following Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel & Blundell (NeurIPS 2017), we train 5 independently initialized, independently optimized UNetFaultNet members (no MC dropout at inference) and decompose predictive Bernoulli variance into epistemic disagreement and conditional aleatoric ambiguity across all 5,167,373 footprint pixels (uncertainty-report.json, phase2-candidate-review.csv).

Variance decomposition

Exact law of total variance for the 5-member Deep Ensemble

For ensemble member $m \in \{1,\dots,5\}$ with seed $73191 + 1009(m-1)$, let $p_m(x) \in [0,1]$ be that member's deterministic fault probability at pixel $x$. Treating the ensemble as a uniform mixture over member identity followed by a Bernoulli outcome yields the exact identity:

p̄(x) = (1/M) Σm=1..M pm(x)
Uepi(x) = Varm(pm(x)) = (1/M) Σm=1..M (pm(x) − p̄(x))²
Ualea(x) = Em[pm(x)(1 − pm(x))]
Utotal(x) = p̄(x)(1 − p̄(x)) = Uepi(x) + Ualea(x)   (max float32 residual across 5,167,373 px: 7.45 × 10⁻⁸)
Epistemic Uncertainty (Uepi)

Ensemble disagreement from missing evidence

High epistemic variance occurs when independently initialized and trained members assign different probabilities to a structure—for example, when 1 m 3DEP lidar is absent and members weight 10 m directional slope, tilt-angle gradients, and multi-scale context differently.

Aleatoric Uncertainty (Ualea)

Ensemble agreement on an intrinsically ambiguous signal

High conditional Bernoulli variance with low epistemic variance occurs when all 5 members agree on an intermediate probability ($p_m(x) \approx 0.5$), indicating an intrinsically diffuse or partially buried structure where all modalities see a moderate signal.

Survey-coverage triage rule

Raising priority in under-surveyed terrain; lowering priority in heavily surveyed terrain

Using our verified `[0, 1]` historical survey-coverage proxy $c(x) \in [0, 1]$ (`data/processed/survey_coverage.npy`, combining 75% USGS 1 m 3DEP lidar survey coverage with 25% OpenEI GDR 1391 field geothermal well/spring sampling density), we compute for every Phase 2 candidate ($\lambda = 0.12$):

A(x) = λ · min(√Uepi(x) / 0.5, 1) · (1 − 2c(x)),     Priority(x) = p̄(x) + A(x)

When high epistemic uncertainty is concentrated in historically under-surveyed terrain ($c(x) < 0.5$, such as the NE 1 m lidar gap), $1 - 2c(x) > 0$ and $A(x) > 0$ treats ensemble disagreement as positive evidence for human-mapper catalogue gaps, raising review priority. Conversely, in heavily surveyed terrain ($c(x) > 0.5$), $A(x) < 0$ treats ensemble disagreement with suspicion and lowers review priority.
