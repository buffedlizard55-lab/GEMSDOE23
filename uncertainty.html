The brief's rule, implemented literally

How survey coverage changes a candidate's priority

High epistemic variance in under-surveyed terrain → a finding

The members disagree because the data does not constrain the answer. Where independent survey coverage is low, that is exactly where a structure can exist and never have been recorded, so the disagreement raises review priority. 2 of the reported candidates fall in this class.

High epistemic variance in well-surveyed terrain → suspicion

If the ground has been flown, lidar-mapped and geologically mapped and the members still disagree, the disagreement is more likely a model artefact than a missing fault, so priority is damped. 7 candidates are flagged this way.

Aleatoric variance never changes priority

Em[pm(1 − pm)] is irreducible noise in the observations — 100 m pixels mixing a 3 m scarp with its footwall, 400 m flight-line spacing, misregistered source maps. Adding members cannot reduce it, so it is reported but not acted on.

Coverage is measured, not assumed

Coverage = 0.5 · (1 m 3DEP lidar validity, band 12 of lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif, ≈0.754 of the footprint per the product's own metadata) + 0.5 · (rank of USGS SGMC structure-line density in a 2.1 km window). Both external rasters were restored and hash-verified this session, and scripts/phase2_candidates.py recomputes the mask from them. Priority never modifies the submitted raster — it is a reviewer-ranking aid only.
